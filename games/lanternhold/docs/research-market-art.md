### 1b. Signatures we must NOT copy

Kingdom: New Lands / Two Crowns (sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Two_Crowns, https://www.pocketgamer.com/kingdom-two-crowns/app-army-assemble-kingdom-two-crowns-is-this-acclaimed-side-scrolling-strategy-g/, https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kingdom-two-crowns/id1477991646, https://gamesgg etc.):
- The monarch on a horse (mount) moving only left/right, with mounts unlocked by gems (griffin, bear...). [V]
- Gold coins as the one currency, carried in a coin bag, and the "coin drop" as the only input (pay a build site, pay a vagrant, pay a worker). [V coins/bag; the drop-to-pay gesture is from my knowledge, U]
- Vagrants who become subjects after you pay, then become builders, archers, farmers by buying a tool. [V roles]
- The "Greed": masked, shadowy creatures that come out of portals at night from both sides, steal coins, and take the crown; losing the crown ends the run. Portals destroyed with a bomb. [V]
- Island campaign with shipwreck repair to move on, challenge islands. [V]
- Walls and towers as the core defence, with archer posts. [V]
- Water reflection strip under the ground and silhouette-heavy dusk palette [U: not in any fetched source; known from general knowledge, treat as signature anyway].
- Day-night cycle with dusk sky colour change, horse breath, rabbits, parallax scroll [V pocketgamer].
- "Pixel NES-like landscape on a single horizontal band", very little UI. [V pocketgamer]

Yes, Your Grace (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes,_Your_Grace, https://apps.apple.com/us/app/yes-your-grace/id6468678719):
- King Eryk of Davern; queen Aurelea; three daughters (Lorsulia, Asalia, Cedani); Prince Ivo; King Beyran of Radovia. Succession-crisis story, forced marriage, invasion. [V]
- Petitioners arrive each turn in the throne room, you say yes or no, supplies are limited. [V]
- Resources: money, supplies, military, with limited generals / witches / hunters. [V]
- Slavic-folklore monsters and arcane practices. Weekly turns. [V]
- Throne room layout (petitioner walks in from the door, king on the throne, counsel on one side) and the banner/ledger UI [U].

Reigns: swipe-left/right card with two choice labels, four meters (church, people, army, treasury), "you die, next king" dynastic loop. [V general; https://apps.apple.com list]

Rule for our design: a king, a kingdom, a night enemy and petitions are genre, not IP. Avoid: a horse-riding coin-dropping ruler, a coin-stealing masked enemy from portals, the crown as lose condition, a royal family with a daughters/succession storyline, a left/right swipe-card UI, Slavic folklore, the reflection strip.

### 1c. "Light as the defence line"

- The Bonfire games: the name and an actual fire exist, but the sources I read (https://www.gamingonphone.com/reviews/the-bonfire-forsaken-lands-review, https://saveorquit.com/?p=60084, App Store text) describe monsters and raiders at night, not a light radius that blocks enemies. No evidence the light is the defence [V absence in those sources; U about the game's internals].
- Kingdom has campfires and torches as scenery and recruitment points, not a defence [U].
- Light-as-barrier exists in other genres: Romestead lets the fallen avoid well-lit areas (https://games.gg/romestead/guides/romestead-night-raids-how-to-defend-your-settlement/) [V, PC survival]; Minecraft torches only stop spawning; 7 Days to Die light attracts zombies [V, https://7daystodie.fandom.com/wiki/Defense_Tactics]; Helsing's Fire (mobile puzzle: torch reveals monsters) and Knight Light (free indie, protect your torch) [V, search snippets only].
- On the iOS store: queries for torch, lantern, light-defence found only flashlight utilities and a few unrated 2026 indie apps (Glowhold, Last Light: A Keeper's Vigil, Fire Keeper: Campfire, Emberkeep) with 0 ratings [V from iTunes; contents U]. Nothing with real traction.
- Verdict: light as a side-scrolling defence resource is fresh on mobile, not owned by anyone with traction. It is not a patent-like claim though: the idea "monsters fear light" is old folklore and PC survival design.

What makes it clearly distinct (ideas, not verified):
1. Fuel is the economy: light is consumed each night; throne decisions spend or save fuel (oil, wax, wood) that farmers, mines or festivals also need. The throne room becomes the logistics screen for the front.
2. Visible, readable rule: enemies stop at the edge of lit ground. Players build a "line" of light posts, like a wall made of radius circles; they plan gaps.
3. Light types differ: torch (cheap, short, blows out in wind/rain), brazier (radius, needs feeding), beacon (long, wakes enemies elsewhere), mirrors or lenses.
4. Enemies react to light: some cannot enter, some are drawn to a brightest point, some snuff lights. This gives variety without coin theft or crown loss.
5. Lose condition not "crown stolen": e.g. the last beacon goes dark / the dawn bell. Win condition: reach dawn count.
6. Dusk-lit art and real per-pixel lighting (normal maps) make the mechanic the visual centre, which neither Kingdom nor Yes, Your Grace does.

## 2. Pixel art pipeline

### 2a. Recraft API facts

Sources: https://www.recraft.ai/docs/api-reference/getting-started, .../endpoints.md, .../styles.md, .../appendix.md, .../pricing.md, .../models/overview.md, .../models/recraft-v3.md, .../tools/image-to-image.md, .../tools/remove-background.md, https://www.recraft.ai/docs/v4-styles-creation-guide.md, https://wavespeed.ai/docs/docs-api/recraft-ai/recraft-ai-recraft-v3

- Base: POST /v1/images/generations. Body fields [V]: `prompt`, `n` (1-6), `model` (default `recraftv4_1`), `size` ("w:h" preferred or exact WxH), `style` (string), `style_id` (UUID), `style_match` (`regular` V2/V3; `flexible`|`precise` V4/V4.1), `response_format` (`url`|`b64_json`|`multipart`), `image_format` (`webp` default | `png`), `negative_prompt` (V2/V3 only), `controls` {`colors` up to 10 [{rgb:[r,g,b], weight}], `background_color`, `artistic_level` 0-5 V3 only, `no_text` V3 only}. Style references (files / urls) not for V4.1 Flash.
- Models [V]: `recraftv4_1` (default), `recraftv4_1_raster`, `recraftv4_1_flash`, `recraftv4_1_pro` (2K), `recraftv4_1_utility(_pro)`, `recraftv4`, `recraftv4_pro`, `recraftv4_styles(_pro)`, `recraftv3`, `recraftv2`; `_vector` suffix = SVG. 
- Pixel art: the new docs list "Pixel art" as a curated style in the V3 and V2 Illustration groups; curated style names apply only to V2/V3. V4/V4.1 have NO curated styles; they use custom styles (`style_id`, or style references, from 1-10 images) [V styles.md]. The docs do not mention a `substyle` field [V]. Third-party sources use the older form: `style: "digital_illustration/pixel_art"` (WaveSpeed docs, Recraft MCP source, Intercom help) [V these quote it; U that the live API still accepts it]. Both forms must be tested once (cost 0.04 USD each).
- Exact bodies to test [U until run]:
  `{"model":"recraftv3","style":"digital_illustration/pixel_art","size":"1:1","prompt":"...","n":1,"image_format":"png","response_format":"url"}`
  fallback: `"style":"Pixel art"` (curated name; docs example uses names like "Photorealism"/"Illustration").
- Custom style from references: POST /styles (multipart `files` 1-10 or `image_urls`, `model` default `recraftv4_styles`, `prompt`, `style_match`...) costs 5 units [V]; V4 generation with it uses `style_id` (cannot combine with references in one request), `style_match: precise` follows the reference closely [V]. Best: ONE strong high-resolution reference (a hand-checked clean sprite sheet) [V creation guide]. Reference adds 5 units per request when sent inline [V]. Style stores the creation model; use matching model [V].
- Sizes [V appendix]: 1K models (V4.1, V4, V4 Styles): 1:1=1024x1024, 16:9=1344x768, 2:1=1536x768, 3:2=1280x832 etc.; 2K Pro: double; V2/V3: 1:1=1024, 16:9=1820x1024, 2:1=2048x1024. Rate limit 100 images/min, 5 req/s.
- Transparent background: no explicit parameter found on the generation endpoint [V absence]. Use POST /images/removeBackground (`file`, `response_format`, `image_format=png`), 10 units; the doc does not state alpha explicitly (U, test) [V]. Prompt for a flat single-colour background as a backup so the python pipeline can key it out.
- imageToImage: model V3/V4/V4.1 raster or vector; `strength` required 0-1; style/style_id only for V3 and `style_match` must be `regular`; V4 styles not accepted [V]. 40 units V3.
- Credit/price per image (1000 units = 1 USD) [V pricing.md]: V4.1 Flash 7; V4.1 35 (Pro 210); V4 Styles 35 (Pro 100); V4 40 (Pro 250); V3 40; V2 22; vector 80 (V3/V4/V4.1), Pro vector 300; removeBackground 10; create style 5. (One third-party tracker disagrees on V4.1 Pro price, ignore.)
- Note: tools/recraft.sh `gen` only passes `style_id`, not `style`; extending it with a style argument is a one-line change for whoever builds this (not done, research only).
- Caveat on resolution: Recraft output is 1024-ish; a 32 px character has to come from a downsample of an image drawn at about 8-16x. AI "pixel art" is NOT pixel-grid aligned; always post-process.

### 2b. Turning AI images into true pixel art (pure python, no PIL)

Known tools for reference: proper-pixel-art (KennethJAllen), unfake (pypi.org/project/unfake), perfectPixel (github.com/harjeb/perfectPixel), Sprite Fusion Pixel Snapper, Pixel Snapper (Scenario), dev.to "How to tame your AI pixel art" (https://dev.to/jenissimo/how-to-tame-your-ai-pixel-art-3pk5), Kopf & Lischinski "Depixelizing" and Gerstner 2012 "Pixelated image abstraction" (https://pixl.cs.princeton.edu/gfx/pubs/Gerstner_2012_PIA/index.php). All [V exist]; behaviour details [U beyond snippets].

Pure-python recipe (PNG read/write via zlib+struct, pixels as lists/bytes):
1. Decode PNG (zlib, unfilter rows) to RGBA arrays.
2. Alpha first: binarize at a threshold (>=128 opaque) after removeBackground; erode 1 px fringe where neighbours are transparent to kill halo colours.
3. Grid detection (needed because the AI "pixel size" is not an integer): compute per-column and per-row gradient magnitude sums, find the dominant period by autocorrelation / FFT-free comparison over candidate sizes 6-24; or skip detection by choosing target size and resampling by area. Easier: ask for an image where the subject's bounding box is cropped, then scale so height = target game pixels * k.
4. Block downscale to target resolution (e.g. 32x48): for each target pixel, take the block of source pixels and pick the DOMINANT (mode) colour after a coarse 4-bit-per-channel quantisation, fall back to the per-channel MEDIAN when no colour has >35% share. Do not use bilinear (blur) or plain nearest (noise). [V concept from unfake "dominant"/"median" modes]
5. Palette quantisation: median-cut on all sprites together (shared palette of 16-32 colours for the whole game) or map to a hand-authored palette (e.g. a dusk palette plus a fire-yellow ramp) by nearest colour in Lab or weighted RGB. A shared palette also fixes cross-image consistency. [V median cut/k-means/octree are standard]
6. Clean-up: remove single-pixel islands (orphan pixel whose 4 neighbours differ from it and are all the same colour -> set to neighbour); remove "jaggies" in outlines by filling pixels with 3 same-colour neighbours; force a 1 px dark outline by colouring opaque pixels that touch transparency with a darkened own colour. Keep alpha binary (no semi-transparent pixels).
7. Optional dithering only for sky gradients: ordered 4x4 Bayer dither with the palette.
8. Export nearest-neighbour upscale only at render time (integer scale), never bake it.
Cost: a 1024x1024 image is 1M pixels; pure python loops take about 2-6 s per image if written with bytes/slices; keep each run under 20 s and process one image at a time (the Mac heat rule).

### 2c. Animation for 24-48 px characters

Options:
- AI frames per animation: consistency fails (different silhouette, palette, proportions each frame) [U general; known from experience]. Not recommended for characters; acceptable for hero key art and maybe for background props.
- Cut-out rig: one AI-generated sprite split into parts (head, torso, arms, legs, weapon) and moved/rotated in code with snapped integer positions. Cheap, consistent, and good enough at 24-48 px if rotations are limited to quarter steps; looks "puppet-like".
- Procedural pixel characters: a few body templates drawn as pixel grids in code, with palette swaps and parts (hat, hair, tool, cloak) for villagers, guests, enemies. Great for crowds and visual variety, zero AI consistency risk.
- Hand-authored grids in code (ASCII art strings of palette indices): best for the 4-6 hero frames that matter (king, the main enemy, throne).
Recommended mix:
- Villagers/guests: procedural template + palette swap + hand-authored walk cycle once for the template.
- King and 2-3 enemy types: hand-authored/AI-assisted grids, cleaned with the pipeline above, then animated with 2-frame offsets on parts.
- Environment, buildings, portraits, throne room background: Recraft in a custom style (recraftv4_styles + one reference) or V3 pixel_art, then pipeline 2b.
- Frame counts (sources: https://dev.to/nitin_808a59e08de0d0385ab/how-many-frames-does-a-sprite-animation-need-24k9, https://www.sprite-ai.art/blog/sprite-animation-frames, asset packs on itch.io) [V as typical]: idle 2-4, walk 4-6 (6 is smooth; 4 is acceptable at 24 px, reuse mirrored for left/right), attack 3-5 (windup, strike, recovery), hurt 2, death 4-6 (6-10 in big packs). A minimal convincing set for an enemy: walk 4 + attack 3 + death 4 = 11 frames; for the king: idle 2 + walk 4 + interact 2.
- Mirror sprites for the other direction (halves the work).

### 2d. Normal and emissive maps

Sources: arXiv "Analysis and Compilation of Normal Map Generation Techniques for Pixel Art" (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.09692, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09692v1.pdf), Laigter docs (https://laigter.readthedocs.io/en/latest/HowTo/normal-map-generation.html), https://www.sprite-ai.art/blog/normal-map-generators-compared.
- Automatic standard: luminance as height, Sobel (3x3) gradient gives dx, dy, normal = normalize(-dx*k, -dy*k, 1), stored as RGB. [V] The paper warns that this "may not yield correct results" for pixel art style; keep smoothing at 0-1 for pixel art [V].
- Pure-python recipe: height = luminance + alpha-based bevel (distance-to-edge via two-pass chamfer distance on the alpha mask, clamp to 2-3 px, gives rounded volume); height = 0.5*lum + 0.5*bevel; Sobel on height; quantise normals to 3-4 levels per axis so lit result keeps the pixel look (avoids smooth gradient on a flat sprite).
- Hand-drawn normals are NOT needed for sprites this small; make exceptions only for the lit props (torch, brazier, beacon, lens) where shape matters; there, set the height by hand as a palette-index to height lookup (say 4 levels).
- Emissive: no standard automatic method [V: none of the sources cover it]. Do it by colour: pixels in the "fire ramp" palette indices (yellow, orange, white) are emissive; a mask is generated from palette index at export. Flames, windows, lantern glass, eyes of enemies use it. Cheap and exact because we control the palette.
- Runtime: in SpriteKit/Metal a simple fragment shader with N point lights (light posts) is enough; do not need a real-time normal-map engine on old iPhones if the budget is tight [U].

## 3. Ads fit (short)

Sources: https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6066980, https://admob.google.ca/home/resources/best-practices-for-in-app-rewarded-video-ads, https://blog.google/products/admob/updated-guidance-on-implementing-admob/ [V]; https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6128543, https://groups.google.com/g/google-admob-ads-sdk/c/soXtLw4IXPc [V snippets].
- Rewarded (opt-in, player presses a button that states the reward): revive after a lost night; double the morning's reward; a merchant's gift at the gate; extra fuel for one night; speed up the building day; a second petition. Always optional, never blocking progress.
- Interstitial only at natural breaks: after the dawn summary / before the next day, not between a petition and its answer, never mid-night, never right after a rewarded ad. Google explicitly says interstitials belong at transitions and that surprising users can cause accidental clicks and loss of ad serving [V].
- Fair feeling: 1 interstitial per 2-3 days at most (suggestion, not a Google number [U]); first 3 days ad-free; show the reward before the player opts in; the player must lose nothing by declining.
- AdMob "low value content" [V snippet]: ads may not run on apps with little or no value and/or excessive advertising. I found no AdMob page with that exact phrase. Risk reducers: real content depth (many unique petitions, several enemy types, progression), no ad spam, no interstitial as the first screen, no incentive that pushes clicks. A game with 100+ distinct petition texts and a real tutorial is safe; a thin template reskin is not. Keep consistent with the Apple 4.3 note in CLAUDE.md.
