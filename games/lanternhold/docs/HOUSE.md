## 1. The house (this replaces the lord's origin in STORY.md)

- **House Lantern is the guardian house.** It descends from the one who carried the Eternal Light into the Cleft a
  thousand years ago, and it has defended that light ever since. The Flame in the hearth is that light.
- **Made or stolen.** The house says its founder made the light. The dark says it was stolen. Chapter 1 keeps both in
  the air. The story's own secret (the Gloom comes for the hearth fire "because it is theirs") is where it leads.
- **The lord is the heir by right,** raised in the house, known to everyone. Court, town and envoys respect the lord.
- **The lord never was a lamplighter.** Nobody calls the lord "lamplighter", a servant, a child or too small for the
  chair. The circlet fits.
- **What stays:** Aldric rode out and did not come back, and the lord wants to know what happened; the lord's short,
  plain voice; the lord has no fixed name (the player types it, `{lord}` in the strings); Pip may keep "Your Wickship" (a fool's joke about the house's name).
- **The lord's fear** is no longer to be found out. It is to be the Lantern on whose watch the light goes out.
- **The enemy's herald** addresses the lord as the heir of a thief. That is the dark's side of "made or stolen".
- **Respect is not distance.** Warmth shows in deeds (a supper kept warm, a cloak mended unasked), never in talking down.
- The town's lamplighter (TOWN.md section 7) is a townsman and a figure of his own. What STORY.md gave the lord as "what
  only a lamplighter knows" (every window, who is awake at midnight) is his: he can be the lord's ear in the town.

## 2. The court is the family

- The people who live in the castle are the lord's family in chapter 1. They let the lord in step by step.
- **They have ties among each other:** at least three threads the player sees without any menu (for example an old
  affection never spoken, a feud over the books, a teacher and a pupil). They play in the world: who stands with whom,
  who leaves when the other comes, a line overheard, an object that changes hands. In two or three moments the lord
  can tip a thread.
- **Closeness is seen and heard, never a number:** what they say to the lord in private, where they stand, small gifts.
- **Chapter 2:** the lord founds a family of their own (a match, later children). Chapter 1 only plants it.

## 3. Rooms one may enter and never must

- **The lord's chamber:** one door off the great hall, a few steps. **The dungeon:** one stair down by the gatehouse.
- Nothing the player needs happens only there. No sleeping to manage, no room upkeep.
- **The court moves:** every court member has a place by part of the day and by day. Sometimes someone is in the
  chamber or at the cell, sometimes in the kitchen, the chapel, on the wall or in the town.
- **A sign in the world** when someone waits: a candle in the chamber's window; the jailer at the head of the stair.
- **The dungeon holds what the lord's judgments put there.** A person the lord had locked up sits there with a face,
  can be heard again and set free. One cell is kept for something from the dark.

## 4. Special evenings

- **Not every day:** about five in chapter 1, on story days or when a special guest stays the night. An ordinary day
  has no evening scene.
- At dusk the guest and the court sit at the table by the hearth. A short scene, under a minute, with two or three
  choices. Then the horn: the night still comes.
- **Kinds:** a guest from the inner lands who weighs us; **the double game** (someone from the other side offers a
  pact, and the lord can play along to learn what is planned, at a price if it comes out); an evening of the court
  (a thread of section 2 turns); the wake after a broken night; the feast when a wealth stage is reached.
- Every choice changes that night or a later day in one way the player sees.
- **The waking picture** (the lord's chamber at first light, someone of the court with the news) is kept for mornings
  after a special night: a broken night, a wealth stage reached, a story day. Never every day.

## 6. The legend: made or stolen (build 2: the strings; build 3: the prologue panel and Hesper's line)

1. **The founder** is Taper, the old lord's younger son, keeper of the great hearth of the old seat beyond the Cleft
   by birth (STORY.md section 3, option A). The town calls him Saint Taper. He was never a lamplighter. **This origin
   is the truth (6.6) and never part of the house's own telling:** the house and its children only know that Taper
   struck the Light from the bare rock (STORY.md section 2, year 1, as the list corrects it). So Dame Orla's "came down
   out of the Cleft, not up to it" stays news, and the court still laughs at her.
2. **The Eternal Light** is the Flame in the hall's hearth, and the Great Beacon is lit from it: "one fire in two bowls".
3. **"Made", as the house tells it:** in the first winter of the dark Taper struck the Light from the bare rock of the
   Cleft and held it there nine nights until the dark broke on it. People gathered to it, and he took no title.
4. **"Stolen", as the dark tells it:** the Light was the hearth of the King's own hall. A son of that hall ran with
   his father's fire and never brought it back. So the herald greets "the heir of the thief", in perfect courtesy.
   **"Thief" is always tied to the fire, never to the chair or the hall** ("by {his|her} borrowed fire" in both
   `herald_demand` and `herald_alone`; "Whose fire is this, herald?" and its answer "His fire. Before the thief.";
   `line.herald_throne` "remembers this fire… in his own hall, a thousand years before yours"). From day 15 the court
   believes the King is Aldric; a "thief" tied to the chair would read as "you took Aldric's seat", which section 1
   rules out.
5. **Why the dark wants it back:** it is theirs. Without it the people left behind thinned into smoke, and they come for
   the hearth every night (STORY.md section 3, unchanged). They never touch a Lantern: the lord is their lords' blood,
   because Taper was.
6. **The truth, never told in chapter 1:** neither. Taper was sent out with it by his father, to keep it alive until
   the old lord called, and kept it. Hesper's secret sentence "The fire is on loan" sits exactly between the two
   tellings; it is kept for chapter 2.
7. **No new plot:** the secret, the King as Aldric from day 15, night 20, the three endings and "Keep the light, and

   Blutes. / {Der Erbe|Die Erbin} des Hüterhauses.“
   **Hesper's bonus, in the hall.** Every dusk the town's lamplighter lights his pole at the Flame and walks out

## 7. The lord's sheet (build 2; replaces STORY.md section 4)

**Who.** Lord Aldric's cousin, the heir by right, about nineteen, orphaned small and raised in the castle as the heir
of the guardian house: the hearth watch at Hesper's side, the lines at Brannock's, the ledger at Odile's. Everyone in
the valley has known that face as the heir's. The player types the name (no fixed name) and picks Burgherr or
Burgherrin.

**Voice (unchanged).** Choice labels only, 40 characters at most. Short, plain, no decrees, no royal "we", no speech
but the one on day 20. Kind without softness, hard without cruelty. Never jokes at a petitioner's cost, never
threatens.

**Wants.** To know what happened to Aldric, the only family there was. To keep the Flame lit, which is what the house
is for. To be worthy of the thousand years.

**The fear.** To be the Lantern on whose watch the light goes out. It is never said aloud but once, as the honest
speech on day 20: "I am afraid too. I stay." / „Ich habe auch Angst. Ich bleibe.“ It shows in deeds: the lord feeds
the hearth before the walls, counts the lanterns back in at dawn, never boasts.

**What only the heir of the guardian house knows.** The Flame's signs (when it leans, what blue means, the black of
three breaths), learned at Hesper's knee. The Count by heart: every hook in the chapel and whose it was, every
lantern number of the garrison. Every key of the castle and the stair nobody uses. The house's words, and the
custom that the heir sits one night alone at the hearth before taking the circlet. Texts use it: the lord knows a
soldier by "lantern thirty-one", a widow by "the hook beside the chapel door". What happens in the town after dark
the lord hears from the town's lamplighter (TOWN.md section 7), who is the lord's ear there. The lord carries the
house's lantern on its hooked staff (the sprite stays; only its name changes).
