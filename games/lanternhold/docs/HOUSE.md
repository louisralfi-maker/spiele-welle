## 1. The house (this replaces the lord's origin in STORY.md)

- **House Lantern is the guardian house.** It descends from the one who carried the Eternal Light into the Cleft a
  thousand years ago, and it has defended that light ever since. The Flame in the hearth is that light.
- **Made or stolen.** The house says its founder made the light. The dark says it was stolen. Chapter 1 keeps both in
  the air. The story's own secret (the Gloom comes for the hearth fire "because it is theirs") is where it leads.
- **The lord is the heir by right,** raised in the house, known to everyone. Court, town and envoys respect the lord.
- **The lord never was a lamplighter.** Nobody calls the lord "lamplighter", a servant, a child or too small for the
  chair. The circlet fits.
- **What stays:** Aldric rode out and did not come back, and the lord wants to know what happened; the lord's short,
  plain voice; the lord has no name in any text; Pip may keep "Your Wickship" (a fool's joke about the house's name).
- **The lord's fear** is no longer to be found out. It is to be the Lantern on whose watch the light goes out.
- **The enemy's herald** addresses the lord as the heir of a thief. That is the dark's side of "made or stolen".
- **Respect is not distance.** Warmth shows in deeds (a supper kept warm, a cloak mended unasked), never in talking down.
- The town's lamplighter (TOWN.md section 7) is a townsman and a figure of his own. What STORY.md gave the lord as "what
  only a lamplighter knows" (every window, who is awake at midnight) is his: he can be the lord's ear in the town.

## 2. The court is the family

- The people who live in the castle are the lord's family in chapter 1. They let the lord in step by step.
- **They have ties among each other:** at least three threads the player sees without any menu (for example an old
  affection never spoken, a feud over the books, a teacher and a pupil). They play in the world: who stands with whom,
  who leaves when the other comes, a line overheard, an object that changes hands. In two or three moments the lord
  can tip a thread.
- **Closeness is seen and heard, never a number:** what they say to the lord in private, where they stand, small gifts.
- **Chapter 2:** the lord founds a family of their own (a match, later children). Chapter 1 only plants it.

## 3. Rooms one may enter and never must

- **The lord's chamber:** one door off the great hall, a few steps. **The dungeon:** one stair down by the gatehouse.
- Nothing the player needs happens only there. No sleeping to manage, no room upkeep.
- **The court moves:** every court member has a place by part of the day and by day. Sometimes someone is in the
  chamber or at the cell, sometimes in the kitchen, the chapel, on the wall or in the town.
- **A sign in the world** when someone waits: a candle in the chamber's window; the jailer at the head of the stair.
- **The dungeon holds what the lord's judgments put there.** A person the lord had locked up sits there with a face,
  can be heard again and set free. One cell is kept for something from the dark.

## 4. Special evenings

- **Not every day:** about five in chapter 1, on story days or when a special guest stays the night. An ordinary day
  has no evening scene.
- At dusk the guest and the court sit at the table by the hearth. A short scene, under a minute, with two or three
  choices. Then the horn: the night still comes.
- **Kinds:** a guest from the inner lands who weighs us; **the double game** (someone from the other side offers a
  pact, and the lord can play along to learn what is planned, at a price if it comes out); an evening of the court
  (a thread of section 2 turns); the wake after a broken night; the feast when a wealth stage is reached.
- Every choice changes that night or a later day in one way the player sees.
- **The waking picture** (the lord's chamber at first light, someone of the court with the news) is kept for mornings
  after a special night: a broken night, a wealth stage reached, a story day. Never every day.
