# Lane i18n: the six languages after English and German

Branch `claude/lh-i18n`, cut from the integration branch (build 86 plus sound). This lane brings French, Spanish,
Italian, Brazilian Portuguese, Japanese and Simplified Chinese to the game: first the story texts
(`Resources/Story.xcstrings`), the interface strings after the rebuild.

## Step i18n-0: the tools and the glossary (14.11.2026)

Scope: prepare the six languages for the story texts (882 keys: petitions, choices, reactions, Odile's counsel,
court lines, speakers, remembered lines, endings; English and German exist). No translation yet, no runner round
(the step says so).

### What exists

All in `games/lanternhold/tools/i18n/` (standard-library Python 3, light, safe to run on this Mac):

- `README.md`: how a translator works (export, one chunk and one language at a time, check to OK, notes, merge) and
  the budget table.
- `glossary.md`: every decision for the six languages, none left open: the voice (two sentences, then what it means
  in each language), the lord or lady through `{a|b}` groups and what takes a group in each grammar, who says "you"
  how (court, narrator, lord, children, Dame Orla), the words of address by speaker as the German distinguishes them
  (town, soldiers, court, old friends, outsiders, Pip, Marek, Hesper, Seren, the herald, Ser Ludo), each voice's
  speech style in Japanese and Chinese, the fixed terms (world, house and customs, titles, court roles, the six
  houses, the seven creatures, defences, smaller recurring words, Ser Ludo's boasts), katakana and hanzi for every
  name, punctuation per language, numbers and money, when the German decides an ambiguous English line, and the
  names still to come (chapter title, the three endings, the endless mode, the Lord / Lady tiles).
- `export.py`: writes `source/` (git-ignored): `story.en.json`, `story.de.json` (key to text, catalog order),
  `chunks.json` (9 chunks of about 110 keys, kinds kept together, never a petition or a speaker's lines split, with
  every key's kind, voice, speaker, petition, days, choice and branch, and the budget table) and
  `chunks/chunk-NN.json` (one file per chunk with English, German, context and the budget per language).
- `check.py <lang>`: every key present and none extra or doubled; `{lord}` and printf placeholders as in English;
  `{a|b}` groups well formed; the budgets (characters, or width units for ja and zh-Hans; `{lord}` 16, a group its
  longer half); no empty text; nothing left in English; the language's punctuation. Warnings for a text far shorter
  or longer than the English, a Romance text that never turns on lord or lady where the German does, and words
  before `{lord}` that change with the name. `check.py de` checks the catalog's German as a self-test: 0 errors.
- `merge.py`: writes finished languages into `Story.xcstrings` only if every one passes `check.py`; English, German,
  other languages, comments and key order stay byte for byte; Xcode's layout; `--dry-run`, `--remove`.
- `i18nlib.py`: the shared rules (kinds by key name, the budgets, the `LordText` placeholder parser, width units,
  who says each key from the petition and line files).

The budgets (`i18nlib.LIMITS`): English and German as `CourtValidator.swift`; the Romance languages 25 % over English
on the scrolling kinds (text 300, counsel 175, reaction 175, line 150, ending 120) and German's numbers on the
one-line slots (choice 48, remembered 56, tile 28, name 24, role 32); Japanese like the Romance languages in width
units, Chinese like German in width units.

### Hooks into existing files

None. Every file of this step is new (`tools/i18n/*`, this file). The join has nothing to re-apply; after the join,
run `python3 tools/i18n/export.py` again so the chunks match the joined catalog.

### Checks run (on this Mac, no build)

- `check.py de` on the catalog: 882 checked, 0 errors, 2 warnings (two German texts at 165 % of the English length).
- Negative tests on scratch translations (fr, es, ja): missing and doubled keys, broken groups, a stray `|`, `{lord}`
  dropped, over-budget texts (Latin and width units), text left in English, English words, Latin letters in
  Japanese, French no-break spaces and quotes, Spanish ¿ ¡, half-width punctuation and spaces in Japanese: each
  caught. Sample texts written to the glossary for fr, es, ja and zh-Hans pass.
- `merge.py` on a scratch copy of the catalog: pt-BR and ja merged (English and German unchanged, checked key by
  key), read back by `check.py --catalog-text`, then `--remove` gives a file byte-identical to the original.

### Open, and what waits for the join

- **The translations themselves**: 6 languages × 9 chunks = 54 batches, one helper at a time (README "For the
  lead"). Next step.
- **Merge with the interface, not before it.** A language that is in `Story.xcstrings` but not in
  `Localizable.xcstrings` makes iOS pick it and show the rebuilt interface in English beside a French story. Merge a
  language's story texts in the same change as its interface strings (after the rebuild's screens settle).
- **A unit test for the six languages.** `CourtValidator` checks English and German only. After the first merge the
  join should add a test that holds every language to `i18nlib.LIMITS` and the placeholder rules (or run
  `check.py <lang> --catalog-text` in the round).
- **Project languages.** After a merge, check on the runner that the build contains `fr.lproj` … `zh-Hans.lproj`
  and that the generated project lists the regions (xcodegen `options` may need them).
- **Default name.** `LordIdentity.defaultName` is "Robin" for every language; Japanese and Chinese want ロビン / 罗宾
  (glossary section 7). Interface step.
- **Source nits, not this lane's to change:** `speaker.farmwife.role` "Farmer's wife" has a straight apostrophe;
  three comments in `Story.xcstrings` still say "the speech the king gave" (ending.speech_*). Translators are told to
  follow the text, not the comment.
- **Screens in every language**: the budgets are for the current cards; the rebuilt cards are judged in screenshots
  of German, Japanese and Chinese (DESIGN.md "Languages and text").
