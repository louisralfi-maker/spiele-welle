# Lanternhold: the story bible

Written 09.11.2026 for the story pass that follows M3. It is the one source for the world, the people and
the secret. DESIGN.md stays the source for rules and numbers; where the two disagree about the world, this
file is newer and wins, and section 9 lists the disagreement.

Fixed by Louis and not open here: the Worldwall with its single cleft; the castle wedged in it; the shadow
land beyond; the valley, the town and a whole continent of other families behind; House Lantern for a
thousand years; the player as Burgherr with a narrow gold circlet, no king and no crown; the families mixed;
the mystery piece by piece; the creatures as lost people.

Names in German, fixed for all writers: the Worldwall = die Weltmauer; the Cleft = der Spalt; the Throat =
der Schlund; the shadow land = das Schattenland; the Gloom = die Düsternis; the Morninglands = die
Morgenlande; the lord of the castle = der Burgherr; the circlet = der Stirnreif; the Lamp Tithe = der
Lichtzehnt; the Returned = die Heimgekehrten; the Count = die Zählung; the Great Beacon = das Große
Leuchtfeuer; the Lightless King = der Lichtlose König; House Lantern = Haus Lantern. Names of people, houses
and towns are never translated.

## 1. Premise and tone

A wall of mountains as high as the sky parts the world, and it has one door: a cleft a bowshot wide, with a
castle wedged in it. For a thousand years House Lantern has held that door against the tall smoke figures
that come out of the shadow land every night, and nobody knows what they are. This autumn the Great Beacon
at the door's far mouth went dark, Lord Aldric rode out to light it again, and he did not come back. The
circlet passed to the last of his blood: his young cousin, who lights the castle's lamps. You are that
lamplighter, the circlet is too big, and twenty nights from now something that wears a crown is coming
through the door.

Tone, in order of weight:

- **Gloomy and cozy.** The dark outside is real and never joked away. The inside is warm: a hearth, soup,
  a fool, a cook who knew you when you were small. The game is the walk between the two.
- **Serious, with humor from the people.** Nobody winks at the player. Pip jokes because he is afraid;
  Odile is dry because she is tired; Ser Ludo is funny because he means every word.
- **Warm people in a cold world.** Every audience is a small human trouble (a plough, a pension, a goat)
  set against the great dark. The small troubles are the reason the door is worth holding.
- **Dread by what is missing.** No gore, no screaming. A window left dark, a lantern that did not come
  back, a word a man cannot say. The creatures are sad before they are frightening.
- **Sparing with words.** An audience is read on a phone between two nights. One picture per text, one
  turn, then the choice.

## 2. The world

### The Worldwall and the Cleft

The Worldwall runs along the western edge of the continent from the ice in the north to the sea cliffs in
the south. Its peaks stand above the weather; snow never leaves them and no bird crosses. It was not built.
It is a mountain range, older than any house, and the people under it speak of it as of the sky.

Nothing with legs or wings gets over it, and that is the whole of the war. The shadows cannot climb it and
cannot go round it, so they come through the one place where the stone is split from summit to root: the
Cleft. It is about a bowshot wide and a long morning's walk from end to end. The castle, Lanternhold, sits
across its eastern end like a cork. Whoever holds the castle holds the door between two worlds.

### The Throat (new, Louis 09.11. late)

West of the castle the Cleft is called the Throat. It is bare rock and nothing else: black granite walls
going up out of sight, a floor of scree and old cart ruts, no tree, no grass, no moss. The sun reaches the
floor for a few minutes at noon. It is dark there at midday and darker at dusk, and sound carries badly.
This is where the nights are fought. The three battle lines, the ruined beacon tower and the warden carved
into the wall all stand on rock.

From the castle wall you do not see the shadow land. You see stone, and at the far end of the stone a
narrow upright slit of grey-violet fog, like a door left ajar. Only when you walk out, past the outer line
to the beacon tower at the Throat's mouth, do the walls fall back and the country beyond open: fog first,
then the tips of dark firs, then a moor, a road, and the first of the statues. A player who never leaves
the wall knows the shadow land as a slit of fog. A player who walks to the mouth has seen a country. That
difference is the point: the land out there is large, and it is earned by going.

This replaces DESIGN.md's "open shadow land directly before the castle". For art and world code it means:
the front from the Gloom's mouth to the gatehouse is the Throat (rock walls as the near and middle depth,
no firs, no moor, no statues of lords inside it), and the shadow-land layers (fir ridge, forest edge in
mist, moor track, ruins, statues) begin at the mouth and show behind the beacon site and through the slit.
The warden statue stays where it is, because it is cut into the Throat's own wall.

### The shadow land

Nobody on this side has a map of it. What is known fits on one page of the house chronicle:

- It is large. Riders who went three days in found no end of it.
- It is a country, not a pit: firs, fog that does not lift, a dead moor, a road with two ruts, ruined
  walls, and statues of robed figures holding stone lanterns, one after another along the road, all facing
  the Cleft.
- The sun sets into it every evening and it is never seen to rise there.
- The Gloom lives in it. By day it lies far back among the firs like weather. At dusk it comes up the
  Throat as a bank of darkness with cold eyes in it, and the figures walk out of the bank.
- Over a thousand years a few hundred people have gone in: patrols, two lords, the curious, the grieving.
  Almost none came back.

Those who did come back are called the Returned. They come back thin and quiet, their lantern flame burns
grey, and they cannot tell what they saw. They try. What comes out is one small household thing, the same
every time, and nothing else. Seren, when asked what is out there, can only say: "A kitchen." Old Hemmet
at the mill went in with a patrol forty years ago and has said one sentence about it since: "The washing
was still on the line." The valley thinks the Returned are a little mad. The player should come to
suspect that each of them is telling the exact truth.

### The valley, the town and the continent

East of the castle the Cleft opens into Wenddale, a green valley in the lee of the Wall: orchards, a
windmill, poplars, and the river Wend with the stumps of an old bridge. The town under the castle is
Tallowford. It began as the chandlers' and oil-pressers' camp that fed the castle's lamps, and it still
smells of wax. The ford is real; the bridge fell before anyone's grandmother was born and no lord has
found the money to raise it.

Beyond the valley lie the Morninglands, so called because the sun rises there: plains, lakes, hills, a
coast, small towns and castles, and the houses of section 5. Most of their people have never seen the
Wall up close and none of them has seen a shadow. To them the Cleft is a tax. Every house of the
Morninglands owes House Lantern the Lamp Tithe, a yearly share of oil, grain, timber and soldiers, and in
return House Lantern asks for nothing else and rules nothing but its valley. The arrangement is a thousand
years old and has never been written down, which is why it is starting to fray.

### House Lantern's thousand years

The house counts its years from the Kindling. Its own account, as every child in Tallowford learns it:

- **Year 1, Taper.** A lamplighter named Taper came to the Cleft with one lit lantern when the dark first
  poured through, set the lantern down in the gap, and did not move. People gathered to the light. The
  fire of that lantern is the hearth fire in the hall, never out since. The town calls him Saint Taper.
  He took no title. The house is named for the thing he carried.
- **Year 118, Lady Oriel the Mason.** Built the hall around the hearth, the gatehouse and the three lines
  in the Throat. Lit the Great Beacon at the mouth from the hearth fire, carried out in a lantern. "One
  fire in two bowls" is hers.
- **Year 412, Lord Edric the Shut.** After a winter the chronicle calls only the Dark Year, he forbade
  anyone to go past the beacon and cut eleven leaves out of the chronicle with his own knife. The stubs
  are still in the binding.
- **Year 703, Lord Corwin the Rider.** Broke Edric's law and rode in with two hundred lanterns to find
  the end of the shadow land. One horse came back. His is the only empty plinth on the road.
- **Year 961 to 980, Lady Ysmay the Patient.** Aldric's grandmother. Held the door through nineteen thin
  years while the tithe shrank, and taught the house to count every candle.
- **Year 981 to 1000, Lord Aldric.** Big, loud, loved. He laughed a great deal. No children. In the
  thousandth autumn the Great Beacon went dark and he rode out to relight it with twelve riders and the
  beacon's keeper.

The house has never worn a crown and its people are proud of it: "a circlet for those who keep a door, a
crown for those who rule lands." The only crown at the Cleft is the one in the dark.

Every Lantern learns a craft of the light before anything else: chandler, glazier, beacon keeper,
lamplighter. That is why nobody found it strange that Aldric's orphaned cousin climbed ladders with a
wick pole, and why nobody thought of that cousin as an heir.

Arms: a gold lantern on teal. Words: "Keep the light."

### Customs

- **The Round.** At dusk the lamplighter lights every lamp of castle and town from a flame taken at the
  hearth. The lord's hooked lantern pole is the lamplighter's pole; the player keeps doing the Round as
  lord, and the town loves and mocks it in equal parts.
- **The horn.** One long call at dusk: gates shut, the night has begun. Three short: a line is lost. It
  is never blown by day and never for joy. One long call at dawn that nobody has to order is the best
  sound in the valley.
- **The Count.** Everyone who goes out past the gate at dusk carries a numbered lantern. At dawn the
  lanterns are counted back in. A lantern that does not come back gets its hook left empty in the chapel
  for a year, and one keep window stays dark for the same year (the `keep_window` custom).
- **The beacon.** The Great Beacon at the Throat's mouth is kin to the hearth fire and can only be lit
  from it, carried out by hand. While it burned, the dark kept its distance from the mouth. It had never
  gone out in the house's telling. Why it went dark this autumn is Seren's to know (section 6).
- **The statues.** When a lord dies, a statue with a stone lantern is set on the moor road beyond the
  mouth, one place further out than the last, facing home, "so the dead keep the road". The house has had
  forty lords. Section 3 says why the number matters.

## 3. The secret

What everyone at the Cleft can see, and what is true in every option: the figures are gaunt, far too
long, made of smoke, with hollow eyes of cold light. They wear torn clothes and carry broken lanterns.
They want the hearth fire and walk past everything else to reach it. They have never laid a hand on a
lord of House Lantern. Nobody knows what they are and nobody says aloud what the rags suggest.

How any secret is told, whichever is chosen:

- **Four steps over the game: feel, suspect, know, choose.** Chapter 1 lets the player feel that the
  figures were people. Chapter 2 lets them suspect whose. Chapter 3 lets them know. The end lets them
  choose what to do about it.
- **Four channels, cheapest first.** What the creatures wear and carry (art, no text). Court lines (one
  sentence, missable). Petitions (a townsperson brings a found thing or an odd sight). Places (the
  statues, the ruins, the beacon tower, the chronicle).
- **Nobody explains.** A hint is a thing seen, never a theory. The court guesses wrong aloud. Only the
  ending panels are allowed to point.
- **Chapter 1 never says** where the figures come from, what the Gloom is, or what the King is beyond
  the court's own belief from day 15: "the dark crowned Aldric."

### Option A: The Locked Door (recommended)

**The truth.** The shadow land is the old country of House Lantern. The house did not come up to the
Cleft from the valley. It came out through it. A thousand years ago one great hearth kept the Gloom, an
old night-fog that thins anyone without fire into smoke, off the whole of that country, and the hearth
was failing. Taper, lamplighter of the old seat, carried a brand of it out through the Cleft to keep it
alive on the far side until his lord called for it, and whoever could run came after him. The lord
stayed with the sinking hearth and his people. Taper kept the light. He never carried it back. His
children held the door from the wrong side, called themselves a house, and four hundred years on Lord
Edric cut the truth out of the book.

The lost are the people who were left, and everyone who has gone in since and outstayed the oil in their
lantern. Without fire the Gloom thins a person until only the memory of the fire is left. They come
every night for the hearth fire because it is theirs. They do not touch a Lantern because the lord is
the blood of their own lords. The Lightless King is not a man. It is an office: the iron crown of the
old country (the crown House Lantern is so proud of never wearing), the mantle, and the snuffer that
puts out stolen lights. The lost set it on whoever of the lord's blood comes back to them. They waited
three hundred years after Corwin for another Lantern to walk in with a living flame. Aldric did. The
house's words have a second half that nobody on this side remembers: "Keep the light, and carry it
home."

**How it opens.**

- *Chapter 1, feel.* Rags in a teal older than any loom in Tallowford. Child-sized sootlings. A brute
  with a watchman's pole. The statues face the Cleft, and a scout counts sixty before the fog while the
  book has forty lords. The beacon tower's door opens west, toward the shadow land. The warden in the
  Throat's wall holds his lantern out toward the east, lighting a way out. The Returned can only say
  household things. Edric's cut leaves. Hesper's "one fire in two bowls". The herald calls the lord
  "lamplighter" and does not mean it as an insult. An old lady of House Lorn says the Lanterns "came
  down out of the Cleft, not up to it" and is laughed at.
- *End of chapter 1, the first big hint.* At the last dawn the Gloom draws back further than anyone
  living has seen. In the beacon's light, for one breath, the figures had faces. And for the first time
  the mouth of the Throat is clear: the road, the statues going on out of sight, and on a hill above the
  firs a second castle, larger than Lanternhold, in ruins, with a lantern cut over its gate.
- *Chapter 2, suspect.* The first walks out. A cold hearth bowl, twin of the one in the hall. The old
  arms: the lantern under a half sun. A Returned in sight of a ruin finds a second word. House Calder's
  ledgers show the first tithes were sent "to the lord beyond".
- *Chapter 3, know.* Edric's leaves, kept all this time in a place the houses of section 5 can open.
  Taper's errand in his own hand. A lit lantern carried by a Lantern is not attacked out there. It is
  followed.
- *The end, choose.* Carry the hearth fire home through the Throat, with the lost walking behind it and
  the castle dark for one night, or keep it and hold the door for another thousand years.

**The payoff.** The first sunrise the shadow land has had in a thousand years, seen from the old hall,
and faces coming back into the smoke. The lamplighter who was never meant to be lord finishes the errand
of the lamplighter who was never meant to found a house.

### Option B: Count Them Home

**The truth.** There is no other people. Every one of the lost is someone the Cleft lost: a thousand
years of patrols, lamplighters, runaways and strayed children. The Gloom is what a person becomes when
their light is given up. The house's own custom makes them: after a year the empty hook in the chapel is
given to someone else and the number is struck from the book. Whoever is struck while still alive out
there thins into smoke. They come every night because the hearth is home, and they claw at the walls
because nobody opens. The Lightless King is the first person the house ever struck from the Count. He
puts out lights because his was put out. And Aldric was struck on the morning the circlet passed: the
player's lordship made him one of them.

**How it opens.** Broken lanterns with numbers that match Odile's old Count books. A sootling that stops
at a lullaby. Seren's flame is grey because her number was struck while she lived. End of chapter 1: in
the beacon's light they have faces, and Brannock knows one by name. Chapter 2: numbers matched to names.
Chapter 3: the first hook rehung, and one figure that does not attack the next night.

**The payoff.** The book is reopened. Each name read aloud and each lantern relit takes one enemy off
the field for good. On the last night nobody shoots; the lord reads the Count from the wall until dawn.

### Option C: The Fire's Shadow

**The truth.** The Gloom is the hearth fire's own shadow. A fire that has not gone out in a thousand
years has never let its night fall, and the night it owes has piled up outside the door. The Flame burns
more than oil: a little of everyone who warms at it. Those who gave most (blessed soldiers, keepers,
lords) end hollow and walk out past the beacon of their own accord. The statues mark the lords who did.
The Lightless King is all forty of them in one mantle, forty lives tall, and the snuffer is mercy: he
wants to put the fire out so the long night can fall and pass.

**How it opens.** Blessed soldiers go grey early. Hesper's order keeps a second, shorter chronicle. The
lord's own shadow grows longer in the hall from week to week. End of chapter 1: in the beacon's light
the King has many faces, and the last is Aldric's. Later: the statues are empty robes.

**The payoff.** Let the hearth go dark for one night and defend the town without it, for a true dawn on
both sides, or keep feeding it.

### Recommendation: A

1. It uses what is already on screen and decided: statues of lords out in the shadow land, ruins of
   older lines, figures that want the fire and never the lord, teal rags, a house that will not wear a
   crown, an enemy that does.
2. It makes the shadow land a place to go to, which is what Louis asked for the same evening: stone at
   the door, a country when you walk out. Later chapters have a direction: outward.
3. It keeps the nightly defense honest. If the lost take the fire, the Morninglands go dark. Both sides
   are right, and the player is never told he has been murdering neighbours.
4. It gives the families a stake beyond money: the tithe, the bell, the chronicle.
5. The player's low start becomes the key. A lamplighter began this and a lamplighter can end it.
6. B is the warmest but turns every arrow into a wrong, which is heavy for a game with an endless mode.
   C makes the hearth and Mother Hesper the villains and costs the game its warm centre.

Kept from B inside A, because they are true there too: the Count, and faces in the beacon's light.
Hesper's ominous voice stays as a false trail toward C. A must never drift into giants, a walled-in
people with wiped memories, or a built wall; see section 10.
